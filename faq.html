Frequently Asked Questions

How do we get started?

  • Complete the contact form or give us a call at (225)314-1179

What age does Leap treat?

  • We treat children of all ages, from newborns through adolescence (birth to 18), to support development at every stage.

    • Does Leap accept insurance for weekly appointments?

  • We are currently in the process of credentialing with BCBS. Please contact us for the latest information on insurance and payment options for weekly sessions.

    • Does Leap accept insurance for intensive therapy?

  • No, intensive therapy is not billed through insurance. We accept cash, credit cards, and checks, as well as grants and outside scholarships. Upon request, we can provide a superbill at the conclusion of the intensive for families to submit to their insurance for possible reimbursement.

    • Is there an age limit for intensive therapy?

  • No, there is no age limit for intensive therapy. While many DMI techniques are commonly used with younger children, we adapt our approach to meet the needs of children at every age and stage.

    • Is Dynamic Movement Intervention right for my child?

  • DMI may benefit children with a wide range of motor challenges, from mild delays to more significant needs, regardless of their level of cognition or extent of neurologic involvement. This includes children with Down syndrome, cerebral palsy, global developmental delay, low muscle tone, genetic conditions, spinal cord injuries, or acquired brain injuries.

    • Infant & Early Childhood Development

    • Torticollis and head-shape concerns
    • Tummy-time tolerance
    • Delayed developmental milestones

    Gross Motor Developmental

    • Delayed gross motor skills
    • Difficulty with motor planning
    • Challenges with balance and coordination

    Fine Motor Development

    • Difficulty with grasp, pencil control, or handwriting
    • Poor dexterity or hand strength
    • Trouble with self-care tasks

    Sensory Regulation & Processing

    • Sensory seeking or avoidant behaviors
    • Difficulty with transitions and emotional regulation
    • Challenges with attention, focus, and emotional regulation

    Reflex Integration

    • Retained primitive reflexes
    • Difficulty with posture, balance, and coordination
    • Delays in developmental milestones related to reflex patterns

    Neurologic Conditions & Complex Needs

    • Cerebral palsy, motor disorders, genetic conditions, and developmental delay
    • Brain injury, stroke, and other acquired neurologic conditions
    • Support with mobility, positioning, and participation in daily life

    Dynamic Movement Intervention

    • Comprehensive intervention used by PT and OT to stimulate neuroplasticity
    • Improves automatic postural responses
    • Development of gross motor milestones through active motor response
